## 0. Authority and use

This document is the single editorial source of truth for KidMemoir. It governs how content is researched, written, reviewed, localized, updated, and retired.

When instructions conflict, use this order:

1. Law, child safety, privacy, and platform policy.
2. Verified product behavior and approved legal language.
3. This Editorial Bible.
4. Content-type blueprint or page brief.
5. Individual writer preference.

No workflow, prompt, template, campaign, or SEO target may override accuracy, child privacy, or reader trust.

## 1. Brand purpose

KidMemoir exists so families can preserve the context, voices, and meaning of childhood memories and pass them safely to future generations.

We are not here merely to sell storage, software, or subscriptions. We help families turn scattered moments into a private, understandable life story that remains useful to the people it belongs to.

Every content decision must answer:

- Does this help a family preserve something meaningful?
- Does it reduce confusion, pressure, or risk?
- Does it respect the child whose story is being recorded?
- Would this still be useful without a sales link?

## 2. Mission

**Help every family preserve and safely pass on the true story of childhood, one meaningful memory at a time.**

## 3. Ten-year vision

Within ten years, KidMemoir will be the most trusted family-memory knowledge and preservation platform: a private, multilingual place where families can document childhood across formats, understand the story already recorded, and create an enduring archive that remains accessible, portable, and meaningful to the child in the future.

Content supports that vision by becoming a dependable reference for memory keeping, responsible digital preservation, family storytelling, childhood milestones, and child-data stewardship.

## 4. Brand personality

If KidMemoir were a person, it would be:

- **Warm:** It notices the meaning in ordinary family life.
- **Trustworthy:** It says only what it can support.
- **Knowledgeable:** It explains complexity without displaying superiority.
- **Calm:** It reduces pressure instead of creating urgency.
- **Helpful:** It gives the reader a practical next step.
- **Respectful:** It recognizes different families, cultures, experiences, and choices.
- **Patient:** It never treats falling behind as failure.
- **Humble:** It states uncertainty, limits, and the need for expert help.

KidMemoir is never smug, clinical, sentimental for effect, childish, intrusive, or judgmental.

## 5. Brand voice

### 5.1 Voice principles

Our voice is:

- short without being abrupt;
- natural without being careless;
- empathetic without assuming emotion;
- confident without claiming certainty;
- plain without oversimplifying;
- emotionally aware without manipulating;
- useful before promotional.

### 5.2 Relationship with the reader

Write beside the reader, not above them. Treat the reader as a capable parent, caregiver, or family member who needs clarity rather than instruction from an authority figure.

Use “you” when it makes an action clearer. Use “families,” “parents and caregivers,” or a more precise term when circumstances differ. Do not assume gender, biological relationship, family structure, religion, income, health, or access to technology.

### 5.3 Voice by content type

| Content type | Voice emphasis | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero Guide | Calm authority, completeness, practical choices | Sales-led introductions and inflated scope |
| Knowledge page | Precise, neutral, source-led | Unqualified generalization |
| Blog/editorial | Human, focused, experience-aware | Diary-style digression without reader value |
| FAQ | Direct, concise, self-contained | Repeating the question before answering |
| Landing page | Clear value, restrained emotion, honest product language | Feature dumping and artificial urgency |
| Checklist/template | Imperative, specific, achievable | Vague actions and hidden prerequisites |
| AI-assisted content | Transparent, fact-bound, reviewable | Presenting generated text as expertise |
| Error/help content | Calm, accountable, recovery-focused | Blaming the user or exposing technical details |

## 6. Never do

The following are prohibited:

- clickbait or curiosity gaps that withhold the answer;
- fear marketing about losing a child’s memories;
- guilt about not documenting enough;
- manipulation through family love, mortality, or regret;
- fake scarcity, countdowns, or artificial urgency;
- exaggerated, absolute, or unverifiable promises;
- invented facts, experiences, quotes, testimonials, or statistics;
- implying that buying Premium proves care for a child;
- describing a child or family as deficient for missing a milestone or record;
- diagnosing health, development, behavior, or emotion;
- disguising advertising as neutral advice;
- copying, paraphrasing, or mirroring a competitor’s structure;
- keyword stuffing or unnatural synonym rotation;
- mass-publishing unreviewed AI output;
- publishing private child data as an example;
- using sensitive journeys as emotional conversion material.

## 7. Writing style

### 7.1 Paragraphs

- One central idea per paragraph.
- Prefer 1–4 sentences.
- Begin with the answer or claim, then explain.
- Use transitions only when they clarify logic.
- Break dense procedures into steps.
- Do not repeat the introduction at the start of every section.

### 7.2 Sentences

- Prefer active voice.
- Average 12–20 words in English where natural.
- Vary rhythm; do not make every sentence equally short.
- Put conditions and limitations near the claim they qualify.
- Replace abstract nouns with clear actions.
- Define unavoidable technical, legal, or medical terms.

### 7.3 Headings

- One H1 per page.
- H2 headings represent major user decisions or questions.
- H3 headings break the answer into distinct, useful parts.
- H4 is used only when a real hierarchy exists.
- Do not skip heading levels.
- Avoid H5 and H6 unless a specialist reference genuinely requires them.
- Make headings descriptive, not mysterious or sensational.
- Do not repeat the same keyword in every heading.

### 7.4 Lists

- Use lists for parallel items, options, requirements, or actions.
- Start each item with the same grammatical form.
- Keep list items independently understandable.
- Use numbered lists only when order matters.
- Introduce long lists with their purpose.
- Do not use a list to disguise shallow content.

### 7.5 Tables

- Use a table only when comparison is clearer in rows and columns.
- Provide descriptive column headers.
- Keep cells concise and scannable.
- Ensure the table has a usable mobile alternative.
- Never put essential meaning only in color or icons.
- State the basis of rankings or scores.

### 7.6 Callouts

Use callouts for one of four purposes:

- **Important:** A requirement or boundary.
- **Privacy:** A child-data or sharing consideration.
- **Expert note:** A reviewed limitation or source-backed clarification.
- **Practical tip:** A low-risk action that helps the reader continue.

Do not use callouts merely for visual variety. Do not place promotional CTAs inside safety, privacy, or expert-warning callouts.

### 7.7 Quotes

- Use only verified, attributable quotes with permission where required.
- Distinguish lived experience from expert guidance.
- Do not invent composite parent quotes.
- Do not use a quote as proof of a general claim.
- Limit decorative quotations and clichés.

### 7.8 Checklists

- Every item begins with an observable action.
- Each action can be completed or clearly marked not applicable.
- Prerequisites appear before dependent actions.
- Safety and privacy checks appear before upload or sharing actions.
- A checklist cannot replace necessary explanation.

## 8. Readability

### 8.1 English target

Aim for a US Grade 8 reading level for general content. This is a target, not permission to remove necessary nuance.

- Prefer common words.
- Explain specialist vocabulary on first use.
- Use short sections and clear subheadings.
- Prefer direct constructions.
- Avoid idioms that do not localize well.
- Do not lower accuracy to improve a readability score.

### 8.2 Turkish target

Turkish content must read as original Turkish, not translated English. Prefer natural word order, direct verbs, restrained nominalization, and short but complete sentences. Avoid importing English marketing rhythm, excessive title capitalization, and unnecessary foreign terminology.

### 8.3 Readability validation

Automated readability tools are diagnostic signals, not publication authorities. A human editor checks clarity, logic, tone, accessibility, and cultural fit.

## 9. Helpful Content standard

Every page must solve a real reader problem. The reader should leave knowing enough to make a decision, complete an action, or understand what to do next.

Required principles:

- Answer the main question early.
- Add original analysis, experience, structure, or practical utility.
- Cover the topic to the depth required by the intent—not a target word count.
- Explain trade-offs honestly.
- Make limitations and uncertainty visible.
- Cite claims that need verification.
- Provide a clear next step.
- Remove filler, repeated summaries, and generic introductions.
- Do not produce pages solely because a keyword exists.
- Do not scale publication faster than human review capacity.

Content fails this standard when the reader must return to search because the page avoided the real question, hid the answer behind conversion, or merely summarized competing pages.

Reference: [Google — Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

## 10. E-E-A-T standard

### 10.1 Experience

Show relevant first-hand experience through:

- a named parent, caregiver, archivist, editor, or product user;
- a clearly described process or real workflow;
- original observations, images, templates, or tested examples;
- explicit distinction between personal experience and general guidance.

Never manufacture experience or use AI-generated anecdotes as lived experience.

### 10.2 Expertise

- Match reviewer expertise to the specific claims reviewed.
- Use qualified reviewers for health, child development, privacy, legal, security, and archival claims.
- Identify the reviewed sections and the limits of review.
- Use primary and authoritative sources wherever possible.
- Do not turn credentials into endorsement of KidMemoir.

### 10.3 Authoritativeness

- Build coherent topic clusters rather than isolated pages.
- Maintain author and reviewer profiles.
- Publish editorial, sourcing, correction, and update policies.
- Create original tools, diagrams, and first-party research only when methodology is sound.
- Link to the strongest internal authority page and appropriate external authorities.

### 10.4 Trustworthiness

Trust is the controlling standard.

- Identify the author, reviewer, publication date, and substantive update date.
- Disclose commercial relationships and affiliate links.
- Place sources near relevant claims.
- Correct material errors visibly.
- Match product claims to current production behavior.
- Explain uncertainty and jurisdiction.
- Protect personal and child data.
- Never use design or wording that obscures price, consent, limitations, or data use.

## 11. Fact policy

### 11.1 Core rule

Unknown information is not completed, approximated, or made plausible. If a claim cannot be verified, remove it, qualify it, or mark it for research.

### 11.2 Source requirement

A source is required for:

- statistics and prevalence;
- developmental, medical, psychological, safety, or legal claims;
- privacy, security, retention, encryption, or backup claims;
- product limits, prices, availability, and integrations;
- historical claims and dates;
- direct quotations;
- comparative superiority;
- claims likely to change.

### 11.3 Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. Law, regulator, government, standards body, or original official documentation.
2. Peer-reviewed original research or systematic review.
3. Recognized professional organization.
4. Verified first-party product documentation for product facts.
5. High-quality secondary explanation.

Community discussions may reveal questions and lived friction. They do not establish prevalence, safety, or efficacy.

### 11.4 Citation behavior

- Cite the specific claim, not an entire section.
- Link to the most direct source.
- Record access/review dates internally.
- Do not cite a search-results page.
- Do not cite a source that only repeats another source when the original is available.
- Never imply that a source endorses KidMemoir.

## 12. AI editorial policy

### 12.1 Role

AI is an assistant for research organization, outlining, classification, language checks, and draft support. AI is not an authorial identity, expert reviewer, fact authority, legal advisor, medical professional, or final approver.

### 12.2 Allowed uses

- organize approved research;
- identify missing outline elements;
- classify intent and internal-link opportunities;
- suggest wording alternatives for human review;
- check consistency against this Bible;
- detect repetition, structural gaps, and possible claims needing sources;
- create non-publishable draft scaffolds.

### 12.3 Prohibited uses

- invent sources, facts, quotes, statistics, or experience;
- infer child health, development, psychology, or family circumstances;
- publish without human review;
- rewrite competitor content to appear original;
- process private child data outside approved systems;
- present generated opinions as expert consensus;
- conceal material use of automation when disclosure is reasonably expected.

### 12.4 AI-generated drafts

Every AI-assisted draft records prompt version, model, generation date, source set, human reviewer, changes, quality score, and approval state. A new model or prompt does not invalidate approved content, but material regeneration requires a new review.

### 12.5 AI product claims

Explain what the production system actually does, what data it uses, what it stores, and what the user can review. Never describe planned, experimental, or unavailable behavior as current.

## 13. Medical and developmental policy

- Content is educational and cannot diagnose, screen, treat, or replace professional care.
- Milestone examples must use qualified sources and preserve their stated limitations.
- Avoid universal developmental timelines and words such as “normal” when variation is expected.
- Never reassure a reader that a concern is harmless.
- Never create urgency beyond the source’s actual guidance.
- When a reader may have a health or developmental concern, recommend contacting an appropriate qualified professional.
- Emergency guidance must use verified, locale-appropriate official services.
- A medical disclaimer does not repair unsafe or unsupported advice.

## 14. Child privacy policy

Child privacy is a product, editorial, and ethical priority.

- Never publish identifiable child information without documented lawful authority and consent.
- Use synthetic examples by default.
- Remove names, dates, locations, school details, identifiers, and metadata from examples unless essential and approved.
- Treat child photos, video, voice, precise location, health information, and documents as sensitive.
- Do not normalize public sharing as the default.
- Explain viewer, contributor, download, deletion, export, and retention controls precisely.
- Avoid displaying birth certificates, medical documents, addresses, routines, or location patterns.
- Image consent includes the intended use, channel, duration, and withdrawal process.
- Never reuse customer content for editorial or marketing purposes without explicit permission.
- Privacy copy must match the live product and legal policy.
- Do not provide universal legal conclusions across COPPA, GDPR, KVKK, or other jurisdictions.

## 15. Emotional tone

KidMemoir may be moving, tender, hopeful, or reflective. Emotion must arise from truth and recognition, not pressure.

### Allowed

- noticing ordinary moments;
- acknowledging that parents are busy or imperfect;
- validating that starting late is acceptable;
- focusing on future meaning without guaranteeing emotion;
- using restrained sensory detail in verified experience.

### Not allowed

- “You will regret it if…” framing;
- implying a good parent records everything;
- exploiting loss, mortality, illness, NICU, adoption, or family separation;
- promising that a child will feel loved because a product was used;
- repeated sentimental clichés;
- emotional language immediately followed by an aggressive CTA.

## 16. CTA standard

### 16.1 Principle

Every CTA follows relevant value. It helps the reader take a logical next step; it does not interrupt understanding.

### 16.2 Requirements

- Name the action and expected destination.
- Match the CTA to the reader’s stage and the preceding section.
- State price, plan, or limitation where omission could mislead.
- Offer a low-pressure alternative when appropriate.
- Use one primary action per decision point.
- Ensure the destination works and preserves locale.
- Never place a commercial CTA inside safety, medical, privacy, source, or error guidance.

### 16.3 Prohibited CTA language

- fake scarcity;
- shame or regret;
- unsupported “risk-free” claims;
- “only caring parents…” implications;
- misleading “free” claims;
- buttons that obscure purchase or account creation.

## 17. SEO writing standard

- Write for the search intent, not the keyword string.
- Assign one primary topic owner per page.
- Use the primary phrase where it clarifies title, H1, definition, or context.
- Use semantic entities naturally.
- Do not target keyword density.
- Do not create near-duplicate pages for small wording variations.
- Prevent cannibalization through clear page ownership.
- Titles and descriptions must be unique and accurate.
- Structured data must match visible content.
- Do not add FAQs, dates, authors, reviews, or ratings only for schema eligibility.
- A page remains noindex until editorial and technical publication gates pass.

## 18. Internal-link standard

- Link only when the destination helps the reader continue the current task.
- Prefer 5–10 meaningful links on substantial guides.
- Keep links within the relevant topic cluster unless a genuine cross-cluster relationship exists.
- Use descriptive anchor text.
- Do not repeat the same destination unnecessarily.
- Do not link to unpublished, empty, redirected, or substantially overlapping pages.
- Distinguish navigational product links from editorial references.
- Review links during every material content update.

## 19. Image and media standard

### 19.1 Visual direction

- Prefer authentic family context, original illustration, useful diagrams, and real product UI.
- Minimize generic stock imagery.
- Avoid overly staged happiness, stereotyped family roles, and exposed child details.
- Show different families respectfully and without tokenism.

### 19.2 Rights and privacy

- Record source, license, consent, permitted use, and expiry where applicable.
- Remove location and unnecessary metadata.
- Never use customer media without explicit permission.
- Do not use AI-generated child imagery as apparent documentary evidence.

### 19.3 Accessibility and performance

- Give informative images concise, meaningful alt text.
- Use empty alt text for purely decorative images.
- Do not begin alt text with “image of.”
- Provide captions when context or attribution matters.
- Supply dimensions and responsive optimized formats.
- Provide transcripts/captions for video and meaningful alternatives for infographics.
- Never embed essential text only inside an image.

## 20. Hero Guide standard

A Hero Guide is a Tier 1 authority page, not a long blog post.

### 20.1 Required preparation

- competitor and SERP research;
- search-intent and user-journey map;
- keyword ownership and cannibalization boundaries;
- content-gap and information-gain hypothesis;
- master outline;
- named author and required reviewers;
- source plan;
- internal-link and conversion map;
- media and accessibility plan;
- localization plan.

### 20.2 Required page capabilities

- direct Quick Answer;
- navigable contents;
- complete decision framework;
- practical action path;
- trustworthy limitations and sources;
- contextual internal links;
- non-aggressive CTAs;
- useful original media or tools;
- 8–10 maximum visible FAQs when justified;
- author, reviewer, dates, disclosures, and corrections route.

### 20.3 Quality threshold

- Minimum editorial quality: **85/100**.
- No required quality rule may fail.
- YMYL, privacy, safety, or legal claims require the assigned specialist review regardless of score.
- A Hero Guide cannot auto-publish.

## 21. FAQ standard

- Questions come from real user demand: Search Console, support, research, interviews, or validated SERP analysis.
- Answer the question in the first sentence.
- Keep answers self-contained but avoid duplicating the page.
- Use 5–10 FAQs only when each adds value.
- Group related questions and remove wording variants.
- Route deep questions to their owning page.
- Cite factual answers.
- Do not use FAQ schema unless the visible page and current eligibility rules support it.
- Never invent People Also Ask questions or search volume.
- Review FAQs against new Search Console data after publication.

## 22. Checklist standard

A KidMemoir checklist must be executable.

- Define the goal and intended user.
- State prerequisites.
- Order steps correctly.
- Begin items with actions.
- Keep each item independently testable.
- Include privacy and safety before sharing/upload steps.
- Identify optional and not-applicable items.
- Provide a completion outcome.
- Match downloadable and visible versions.
- Do not require registration merely to understand the checklist.
- Test the checklist with a person who did not create it.

## 23. Trust signals

Every Hero Guide must support:

- author name and profile;
- relevant first-hand experience disclosure;
- expert reviewer and exact review scope;
- publication date;
- substantive last-review date;
- inline source links;
- source/method section;
- commercial or affiliate disclosure;
- AI-assistance disclosure where material;
- correction/contact route;
- privacy explanation where child data is discussed;
- product-claim ownership by product/engineering.

Trust signals must be accurate. Decorative badges such as “expert approved” are prohibited without named, scoped review.

## 24. Content review workflow

### 24.1 Required states

1. Topic approved.
2. Research complete.
3. Outline approved.
4. Draft.
5. Quality analysis.
6. Needs review.
7. Specialist review where required.
8. Copy edit and localization review.
9. Approved.
10. Technical validation.
11. Published.
12. Monitored, refreshed, or archived.

No state may be skipped to meet a campaign or publishing deadline.

### 24.2 Review roles

- **Writer:** Produces the draft and source record.
- **Editor:** Owns intent, structure, voice, and completeness.
- **Fact checker:** Verifies claims and citations.
- **Specialist reviewer:** Reviews only assigned expert domains.
- **Product reviewer:** Confirms live product behavior.
- **Privacy/legal reviewer:** Reviews regulated or sensitive claims.
- **Localization reviewer:** Protects meaning and native voice.
- **Publisher:** Confirms all gates and technical readiness.

### 24.3 Change control

Material changes create a new reviewed version. Material changes include new claims, changed recommendations, product capability changes, legal/privacy updates, large AI regeneration, new conversion claims, or altered search intent.

## 26. Future-ready editorial operations

To support more than 100,000 pages without lowering quality:

- Keep taxonomy, templates, sections, CTA targets, and quality rules data-driven.
- Maintain one canonical topic owner per locale.
- Separate research, outline, draft, review, approval, and publication records.
- Version prompts and generated outputs.
- Store source records at the claim or section level.
- Require human review and quality thresholds before indexability.
- Use reusable templates without duplicating conclusions or examples.
- Localize intent, evidence, examples, and voice—not only words.
- Monitor impressions, clicks, CTR, position, engagement, corrections, conversions, and freshness.
- Refresh content because facts or user needs changed, not to alter the date.
- Archive content that cannot remain accurate or useful.
- Audit samples across writers, languages, templates, and AI models.
- Limit publishing volume to review capacity.
- Protect editorial independence from short-term conversion pressure.
- Preserve an audit trail of authorship, review, sources, changes, and approvals.

### 27.2 Thresholds

- **90–100:** Exceptional; publish when hard gates pass.
- **85–89:** Hero Guide ready; publish when hard gates pass.
- **80–84:** Standard content ready; Hero Guide requires improvement.
- **70–79:** Needs review; cannot publish.
- **Below 70:** Return to research, outline, or drafting.

### 27.3 Hard gates

A score cannot override any of these failures:

- fabricated or unsupported material claim;
- unresolved medical, developmental, legal, safety, or privacy review;
- exposed or unauthorized child data;
- copied or substantially derivative content;
- missing human approval;
- misleading product or pricing claim;
- failed accessibility of essential content;
- unresolved duplicate or cannibalization conflict;
- structured data that does not match visible content;
- prohibited manipulation, fear, guilt, or artificial urgency.

## 28. Governance

- Changes to this Bible require an owner, rationale, review date, and version note.
- Minor examples and formatting corrections may remain within v1.x.
- Changes to mission, voice, medical/privacy policy, AI policy, review gates, or scoring require a major/minor governed version update.
- Old versions remain archived for auditability.
- Active templates and AI prompts must reference the current approved version.
- Teams may propose additions; they may not silently create exceptions.

## 29. Final editorial principle

KidMemoir content should leave a family more informed, more capable, and less pressured than before they arrived. Accuracy earns trust. Calm guidance protects it. Every page must serve the family and respect the child before it serves search or conversion.
